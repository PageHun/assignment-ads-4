Task 4
Consider the Scottish road network shown below. Use the shortest-path algorithm to determine the shortest path from
Edinburgh to Dundee.

Answer: Edinburgh-Stirling-Penth-Dundee, total distance: 50+40+60=150

Explanation.
It's Dijkstra's algorithm. It only works with non-negative weights.
Edinburgh has 3 adjacent vertices Glasgow, Stirling and Perth. From
Edinburgh to Glasgow we pass 70 km. From Edinburgh to Stirling we pass
50 km. From Edinburgh to Perth we pass 100 km. In Dijkstra's algorithm
we always choose the vertex has the smallest edge. In this case, we
would go to Stirling. Now, Stirling has 3 adjacent vertices Glasgow,
Perth and Edinburgh. From Stirling to Glasgow we pass 50km, so if we
get to Stirling trough Glasgow from the start, it would take 120km.
It means, we still get to Stirling from Edinburgh directly. To get
to Perth it takes 40 km, therefore, we would choose road to Perth.
Perth has also 3 adjacent vertices Stirling, Edinburgh and Dundee.
From Perth to Edinburgh we pass 100km. We got to Perth by passing
only 90km, that's why we are still getting to Perth trough Stirling
from the start. From Perth to Dundee it takes 60km. In conclusion,
we get to Dundee by passsing trough Stirling and Perth, and the
smallest amout of distance is 150km.
